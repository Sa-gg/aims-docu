# Additional RRL Insertion Plan for Chapter 2

Status: Planning only. No document edits applied yet.

Purpose:
This file combines both requested outputs:
1. A shortlist of the best additional RRL sources to insert into Chapter 2.
2. A v19 no-edit insertion plan showing exactly which current Chapter 2 paragraphs each source should strengthen.

Selection Rules Used:
- Year must be within 2021-2026.
- Source must come from a reputable academic database or publisher page surfaced through Google Scholar, PubMed, ACM, Springer, BMC, or comparable indexed venues.
- Source must align with the actual A.I.M.S. system lifecycle or objective logic, not just the broad topic.
- Priority was given to Objectives 1.4 and 1.5 because they were the weakest direct-evidence sections in v18.

## 1. Priority Shortlist to Actually Insert

### Priority 1: Objective 1.4
Source:
Hegde, S. V., Shetty, P. P., Senthilkumar, M., et al. (2024). Bridging knowledge gaps: impact of remedial classes on first-year medical students in biochemistry - a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education, 24, 1375. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06243-y

Why this is high priority:
- Directly supports early identification of underperforming students.
- Directly supports tailored remediation based on learning gaps.
- Explicitly mentions quizzes, assignments, quick revisions, and targeted remedial methods.
- This is the strongest new source for Objective 1.4 because it matches the intervention lifecycle better than the current evidence.

Best use in A.I.M.S.:
- Strengthen the remedial workflow rationale.
- Support Trigger, Gap Analysis, and Student Re-Assessment.
- Keep claims framed around tailored remediation and teacher-supported intervention, not automatic AI generation by itself.

### Priority 2: Objective 1.5
Source:
Toti, G., Chen, G., & Gonzalez, S. (2023). Teaching CS1 with a Mastery Learning Framework: Impact on Students' Learning and Engagement. ITiCSE 2023. https://doi.org/10.1145/3587102.3588844

Why this is high priority:
- Directly supports mastery learning through multiple attempts.
- Directly supports separate proof of mastery by unit or topic.
- Directly supports progression based on demonstrated competence rather than one-time completion.
- This is one of the best available additions for defending progression control in A.I.M.S.

Best use in A.I.M.S.:
- Strengthen the logic behind configurable progression locks.
- Support repeated attempts, competency-based sequencing, and progression after mastery.
- Present A.I.M.S. locks as a system design implementation of mastery learning principles.

### Priority 3: Objective 1.1
Source:
Simon, P. D., Jiang, J., Fryer, L. K., King, R. B., et al. (2025). An Assessment of Learning Management System Use in Higher Education: Perspectives from a Comprehensive Sample of Teachers and Students. Technology, Knowledge and Learning, 30, 741-767. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10758-024-09734-5

Why this is high priority:
- The abstract explicitly supports LMS use for information dissemination, giving assignments, providing feedback, and classroom management.
- This strengthens the exact portal workflow that is currently only partially supported in Objective 1.1.
- It is especially useful for defending submission and teacher-side workflow language.

Best use in A.I.M.S.:
- Strengthen Content Publishing, Task Submission, and Teacher Collection.
- Support the student portal as a functional course delivery environment, not just a file repository.

## 3. Recommended v19 Insertion Plan

Note:
The paragraph targets below are identified by their opening lines in v18 so they can be found reliably even if exact Word paragraph numbers shift.

### A. Objective 1.4 Insertion Plan
Target subsection:
1.4 Dynamic remedial quiz generator for targeted student intervention

Target paragraph in v18:
Paragraph beginning with:
"Dynamic remediation is a core element of mastery-oriented instruction..."

Planned source to insert:
Hegde et al. (2024)

Planned role of the source:
- Add direct evidence that tailored remediation improves performance.
- Support the idea that identified underperformers benefit from targeted intervention tools such as quizzes, assignments, and focused review.

Planned wording direction:
- Keep Persky and Hughes (2022) as the pedagogical foundation.
- Add Hegde et al. (2024) as newer empirical support for targeted remediation after identifying learning deficiencies.
- Do not use Hegde et al. to claim AI generation directly.

Suggested placement:
- After the sentence citing Persky and Hughes (2022), or
- As a new middle sentence before the existing Jumao-as et al. (2025) local evidence sentence.

Expected benefit:
- Objective 1.4 becomes more defensible because it will no longer rely mostly on general mastery/remediation theory.

### B. Objective 1.5 Insertion Plan
Target subsection:
1.5 Mastery-based learning module with configurable progression locks

Primary target paragraph in v18:
Paragraph beginning with:
"Mastery-based learning is a pedagogical model grounded in the principle..."

Planned source to insert:
Toti et al. (2023)

Planned role of the source:
- Add direct evidence for multiple attempts, topic/unit-based mastery, and progression after competence is demonstrated.
- Strengthen the claim that progression controls in A.I.M.S. are grounded in real mastery-learning implementation patterns.

Planned wording direction:
- Keep Persky and Hughes (2022) for the broad mastery-learning theory.
- Add Toti et al. (2023) to support operational mastery behavior such as repeated attempts and separate proof of mastery for each unit.
- Continue framing A.I.M.S. locks as implementation design, not as a feature copied verbatim from the cited system.

Suggested placement:
- After the sentence explaining that mastery learning requires performance thresholds and retesting, or
- As a new sentence immediately before the current sentence that begins "These principles provide the pedagogical basis..."

Expected benefit:
- Objective 1.5 gains a more concrete implementation-oriented support source.

### C. Objective 1.1 Insertion Plan
Target subsection:
1.1 Centralized learning portal with material distribution and task submission

Primary target paragraph in v18:
Paragraph beginning with:
"Web-based module management systems address access and distribution constraints..."

Planned source to insert:
Simon et al. (2025)

Planned role of the source:
- Strengthen the link between LMS use and assignment distribution, feedback, and classroom management.

Planned wording direction:
- Keep Dahal and Manandhar (2024) for general LMS structure and resource management.
- Add Simon et al. (2025) to support active LMS functions such as assignments, feedback, and teacher-student course management.

Suggested placement:
- After the first sentence of the paragraph, or
- As a second sentence before the current Dahal and Manandhar discussion of success factors.

Expected benefit:
- Objective 1.1 becomes stronger on the task submission and teacher collection workflow, not just on content access.
